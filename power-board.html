Overview

UBC Thunderbots builds fully autonomous soccer robots for the RoboCup Small Size League. The power board takes the raw battery voltage and turns it into everything the robot needs: regulated logic and motor rails, current telemetry, and the high-voltage charging circuit that powers the robot's kicker and chipper. Under sustained match play it has to do all of that without overheating or dropping out — heat and marginal reliability are exactly what take a robot off the field.

I did a significant share of the board's layout and am now working the v3.6 revision.

Architecture

What's on the board

  • Regulation — DC-DC buck converters step the battery down to the 12 V and 5 V rails (LM22677-based, designed against TI WEBENCH and validated in simulation).
  • Current & power telemetry — INA226 monitors read rail current and power over an I²C bus, so the robot can watch its own power draw.
  • Kick / chip charging — a flyback converter charges a high-voltage capacitor bank that drives the kicker and chipper solenoids, with a dedicated HV-measurement path and an auto-discharge safety circuit.
  • Peripheral interfaces — break-beam ball detection, the Geneva dribbler mechanism, and an MCU breakout, all brought out on the board.

My work on v3.6

  • Completed a large portion of the multi-rail board layout in Altium.
  • Working the v3.6 revision to resolve overheating hotspots and reliability issues — improving thermal handling around the regulators and HV section and fixing signal-integrity problems (e.g., I²C trace routing) so the board holds up under sustained load.
