Power Board (v3.6)

The power and distribution board for UBC Thunderbots' autonomous soccer robots — it runs the whole robot's power, from a battery system and low-voltage logic rails up to a 240 V kicker supply.

Role: Simulation · design · layout — v3.6 revision
Team: UBC Thunderbots (RoboCup Small Size League)
Tools: Altium · LTSpice · Cadence

Overview

UBC Thunderbots builds fully autonomous soccer robots for the RoboCup Small Size League. The power board is the heart of the robot's electrical system: it takes the battery and generates every voltage the robot needs — logic rails, motor power, and a high-voltage supply for the kicker. It has to deliver all of that reliably, in a tight stacked form factor, without overheating under match load.

Architecture

What's on the board

  • Battery & power levels — takes the robot's battery and generates multiple regulated rails: 12 V and 5 V for power, plus a 3.3 V logic rail used by the IO board.
  • High-voltage kicker — a flyback converter charges a capacitor bank up to 240 V, which is dumped through a solenoid to kick the ball, with high-voltage sensing and an auto-discharge safety circuit to bleed the caps down when idle.
  • Current & power telemetry — INA226 monitors read rail current and power over an I²C bus, so the robot can watch its own power draw.
  • Peripheral interfaces — break-beam ball detection, the Geneva dribbler mechanism, and an MCU breakout.

My work

  • Simulation — modelled and validated the power stages in LTSpice before committing to hardware.
  • Design — contributed to the circuit design in Cadence.
  • Layout — did the board layout in Altium, designing it as a stacked board structure to fit the robot's tight footprint.
  • v3.6 revision — resolving overheating hotspots and reliability issues so the board holds up under sustained load.
