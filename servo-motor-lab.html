Servo Motor Control System

A closed-loop DC-motor speed controller built from scratch out of discrete logic and analog electronics — a hands-on mixed-signal control system.

Role: Design, build & test (individual)
Course: ENPH259 (Experimental Techniques), UBC — final lab
Type: Feedback control · discrete digital + analog

Overview

For the ENPH259 final lab I designed, built, and tested a servo control loop that actively regulates a DC motor's speed. It's a genuine mixed-signal control system: the motor's speed is measured on the digital side (an optical tachometer feeding a counter, latch, and DAC), while the regulation happens on the analog side (an op-amp error amplifier driving the motor through a power transistor). A potentiometer sets the target speed, and the loop holds the motor there.

The control loop

How the loop works

The optical slot-sensor puts out one pulse per hole in a wheel on the motor shaft, so its pulse rate is the motor's speed. A timing generator built from Schmidt-trigger delay circuits latches the running count into a D-latch and then resets the counter every interval; an R-2R DAC turns that latched count back into an analog voltage. The op-amp error amplifier compares that measured-speed voltage against the setpoint potentiometer and drives the motor through the power transistor — closing the loop so the motor holds the set speed.

Subsystems I built & tested

  • Latch & reset generator — Schmidt-trigger (CD74HC14E) delay circuits producing correctly-timed latch and reset pulses; I characterized the RC delays against the trigger's hysteresis.
  • Counter & D-latch — two 4-bit counters (74HCT393) cascaded into an 8-bit counter, latched by an octal D flip-flop (74HCT273); debugged a stuck count down to a single bad bit line.
  • 8-bit R-2R DAC — converts the latched count to an analog speed signal.
  • Motor sensor & driver — H21A3 optical interrupter as the tachometer; MJF122 Darlington power transistor (with flyback diode) as the motor driver.
  • Error-signal amplifier — a UA741 op-amp comparing setpoint vs. measured speed; I derived its transfer function and verified the response on the scope.

Results

I characterized the closed loop by sweeping the setpoint and reading back the motor speed. Speed tracked the setpoint voltage linearly, and the motor topped out around 4200 rpm.
