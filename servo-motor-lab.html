Servo Motor Control System

Characterizing a servo as a closed-loop, mixed-signal control system — analog drive and position sensing, with digital acquisition and analysis.

Role: Measurement, modelling & analysis
Course: ENPH259 (Experimental Techniques), UBC — final lab
Focus: Feedback control · mixed-signal signal chain

Overview

A servo is a control system before it is anything else: a motor, a position sensor, and a controller wired into a feedback loop that continuously drives the output toward a commanded target. It is also inherently mixed-signal — the controller and data acquisition live in the digital domain, while the drive to the motor and the position feedback are analog. That split is exactly the kind of signal chain I work on, which is why I framed this lab around the control system rather than the motor alone.

For the ENPH259 final lab I characterized the servo end to end: driving it, measuring how it responds, and modelling that behaviour as a closed-loop system.

The control loop

What I did

  • Drove and measured the servo — commanded the motor and captured its response, treating the servo as a system with an input (command) and an output (position).
  • Characterized the response — analyzed the motor's behaviour (how quickly and accurately it tracks a command, and how it settles) to pin down the parameters that describe the loop.
  • Modelled the control system — related the measured behaviour back to the closed-loop feedback model, reasoning about stability and steady-state tracking.
  • Worked up the data in Python — processed and visualized the measurements with NumPy, pandas, and Matplotlib in a Jupyter notebook.

Why it's mixed-signal

The interesting part of a servo sits at the boundary between domains. The command and the control law are digital; the drive to the motor is an analog (PWM) power signal; and the position feedback is an analog measurement that has to be conditioned and digitized before the controller can act on it. Characterizing the loop means understanding both sides of that boundary at once — the same analog-plus-digital thinking that underpins mixed-signal design.
